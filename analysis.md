## 2. Problems They're Trying to Solve with This Listing

### a) Scaling Engine Capacity for a More Ambitious Game

The Divinity Engine was built and refined across DOS, DOS2, and BG3. For the new project — which promises "greater
breadth & depth" — the engine will need significant extension. The listing explicitly mentions **networking, physics,
animation, and ECS** — all systems that would need rework or expansion for a game designed to push beyond BG3.

**The internship is a capacity play:** they need more hands on deck to extend the engine while senior engineers tackle
architecture decisions.

### b) Building a Talent Pipeline

Larian currently lists **5+ engine programming positions** across studios (Lead Engine Programmer in Warsaw & Quebec,
Senior in Quebec, Engine Programmer in Barcelona & Warsaw). An internship is a low-risk way to evaluate junior talent
before offering full-time roles. The strict portfolio requirement (personal C++ projects, not coursework, less than 2
years old) is a filter for **genuine passion and self-motivation** — they want people who code because they love it, not
because it's a requirement.

### c) Knowledge Transfer & Maintainability

The listing emphasizes *"well tested and maintainable code"* and *"willingness to experiment and iterate."* This
suggests the engine team may be facing technical debt or documentation gaps after the BG3 crunch, and needs engineers
who can help refactor and solidify systems while building new ones.

## 3. Potential Concerns & Risk Factors

### [WARNING] Crunch Culture Risk

Larian is known to have had **intense crunch periods**, particularly during BG3's final development phase. Swen Vincke
has openly discussed the personal toll. As an intern, you may be brought into a team that's still recovering from that
pace or heading into another crunch for the new project. This is the single biggest thing to ask about in an interview.

### [WARNING] Internship ≠ Job Security

This is a **temporary, fixed-term position**. There's no guarantee of conversion to full-time. The strict portfolio
requirements (personal projects only, no coursework) mean they're selective — but also that they expect interns to be
productive quickly, not just learning.

### [CAUTION] Barcelona Relocation

The position is **on-site in Barcelona**. If you're not already based there, you'd need to relocate. The listing doesn't
mention relocation assistance. Barcelona is a great city, but the cost of living and visa/work permit logistics (if
applicable) are real considerations.

### [CAUTION] Tencent Minority Stake

Tencent owns a minority stake in Larian. While Larian remains independent and this hasn't affected their creative
direction so far, it's worth noting for anyone uncomfortable with Chinese tech company involvement. This has been public
since ~2023 and hasn't caused issues, but it's a factor for some.

### [WARNING] "New Projects" = Unannounced, Uncertain

You'd be working on a project that hasn't been fully revealed. Scope, timeline, and even direction could shift. The
*Divinity* announcement was cinematic only — no gameplay, no release date, no details. Working on unannounced projects
can be exciting but also stressful.

### [GOOD] Privately Held = No Shareholder Pressure

As a private company, Larian isn't driven by quarterly earnings calls. They can focus on making the best game possible.
This is a positive signal for job stability — they don't need to hit unrealistic targets.

### [GOOD] Proven Track Record

Baldur's Gate 3 is one of the most acclaimed games ever made. The Divinity series has a loyal fanbase. The company knows
how to make great RPGs. As an intern, you'd be learning from people who literally just won every major game award.

### [GOOD] The Technical Stack is Excellent

C++, ECS, custom engine, networking, physics, animation — this is **real systems programming**, not just gameplay
scripting. The skills you'd build here are highly transferable across the entire game industry (and beyond — ECS and
data-oriented design are used in high-performance computing, simulations, etc.).

## 4. Should You Apply?

### Apply if:

- You have **personal C++ projects** (not coursework) you're proud of and can share source code for
- You're genuinely passionate about **engine-level programming** — memory, performance, ECS, networking
- You want to work on **AAA-quality RPGs** with a proven team
- You're comfortable with **on-site work in Barcelona** (or already live there)
- You're okay with the possibility of **crunch** in exchange for working on prestigious projects
- You see this as a **stepping stone** — even if it doesn't convert to full-time, the experience and name on your resume
  are valuable

### Think twice if:

- You don't have a qualifying portfolio (the listing is strict: no coursework, <2 years old, source code required)
- You're not willing to relocate to Barcelona
- You're risk-averse about working on unannounced projects with uncertain timelines
- You're sensitive to crunch culture or work-life balance concerns
- You need a guaranteed long-term position (this is an internship)

### Questions to Ask in an Interview:

1. **Which project (s) would I be working on?** (Divinity? Engine maintenance? Both?)
2. **What does the team's current work-life balance look like?** (Directly address crunch)
3. **Is there a path to full-time conversion?** What percentage of past interns were hired?
4. **What's the onboarding/mentorship structure** for interns?
5. **What specific engine systems would I own or contribute to?** (Get concrete — ECS? Networking? Animation?)
6. **What's the team size and structure** in the Barcelona engine group?

## 5. Bottom Line

This is a **high-prestige, high-risk, high-reward** opportunity. Larian Studios is one of the most acclaimed RPG
developers in the world, freshly coming off the most award-winning game in recent history, and actively building their
next ambitious project. The engine team is the backbone of everything they do — and they're hiring.

The internship is clearly designed to feed into the **Divinity project** and build junior engine capacity. If you have
the C++ portfolio and the appetite for systems programming, this could be an exceptional career launchpad. Just go in
with your eyes open about crunch, the temporary nature of the role, and the uncertainty of working on an unannounced
title.

**Verdict:** Strongly consider applying if you meet the portfolio requirements and are comfortable with the Barcelona
relocation. It's a rare chance to learn from the best in the business.
