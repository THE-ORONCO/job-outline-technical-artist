# Reference Portfolios — Technical Artists

A curated list of real technical-artist portfolios found on the web, grouped by **estimated seniority**. Use these as
visual/structural references while building
your own tech-art portfolio.

> **How to read this**
> - Seniority is *estimated* from each site's own copy (years of experience,
>   titles held, shipped games, job titles). It is not an official ranking.
> - "Focus" notes the skills the portfolio leans on (shaders, VFX, rigging,
>   tooling, pipeline, procedural, etc.), which matters because tech art is a
>   broad discipline — match references to the job you target.
> - Links were gathered from web search and may change. Verify before relying on
>   any single site.

## Takeaways for your own tech-art portfolio

1. **Pick a target.** Tech art is broad — shaders, VFX, rigging, tools, pipeline,
   procedural. Reference portfolios above are strongest when they clearly lean
   into one or two specialisms (see Zed Poirier, Christian Santori).
2. **Show the machinery, not just the result.** The best references (Pavel Zosim,
   Lei Wu, Richard Katz) explain *how* and *why*, with measurable outcomes (frame time, load time, manual steps
   removed).
3. **Video > stills for tools.** Tools are interactions — short clips or GIFs
   communicate more than screenshots.
4. **Curate ruthlessly.** Your portfolio is only as strong as its weakest piece.
5. **Match the format to the seniority you're targeting.** Junior: 3 tight pieces.
   Senior/Lead: emphasis on scope, impact, and systems that survive without you.
